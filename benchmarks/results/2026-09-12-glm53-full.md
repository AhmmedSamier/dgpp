## The memory headroom revisited (2026-09-12, late)

`kMemoryHeadroomBytes` was 8 GiB, a constant covering what the plan does not
itemize. Measured on the 64K MTP world with 2-second samplers on all four
ranks (node used memory = MemTotal − MemAvailable, minus the idle baseline,
minus the plan's 106.68 GiB; records in `build-ci/fabric-runs/glm53_mem_2026-09-12/`):

| moment | rank 0 | ranks 1–3 |
|---|---|---|
| listening (idle) | +5.96 GiB | +5.52 to +5.59 |
| during a 33.7K-token prefill (428 s) | +5.96 | +5.48 to +5.58 |
| four live requests | +6.00 | +5.50 to +5.61 |

The excess is fixed: it does not move with the context or the load (the
plan's scratch items cover those). Of it, ~1 GiB is the process before the
check (already outside the budget the check reads), 2.22 GiB the loader's
pinned staging mirror (`cudaHostAlloc`, the largest of a layer and the
globals, kept for the process's life by the shared resident stream), and
the rest — cuBLAS handles, the CUDA runtime's 2.3 GiB of shared mappings,
the service's tables — 2.4–2.8 GiB.

Changes: the resident stream's `release_sources()` frees the staging mirror
together with the checkpoint mappings; the GLM-5.3 model materializes its
stack in its constructor and releases right there, before its caches exist
and before any collective is in flight, so the plan counts the staging only
beyond what the caches replace (zero here); every resident family's plan
names the staging; the headroom is 5 GiB. An automatic release at the last
materialized layer was tried and withdrawn: for the lazily loading families
it lands mid-forward, where `cudaFreeHost` waits on the bus's persistent
kernels for one 5 s watchdog period (the loopback worlds showed exactly
that gap), so Qwen and GLM-4.7 keep their mirror as before. Verified on the same world: the node
settles 2.3–2.4 GiB lower (rank 0 +3.5 GiB over the plan at listening, of
which ~1 GiB pre-check), the boot is unchanged (30.5 s), the log shows the
release before "model constructed".

| shape under the 5 GiB headroom | plan | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 96K bf16 + MTP (`mtp1`) | 109.56 GiB | fits, 1.0 GiB spare |
| 112K bf16 plain (`plain`) | 109.25 | fits, 1.3 spare |

## The decode batch's cap lifted to sixteen rows (2026-09-13, 06:09–06:32 and the rerun after it)

Plan D9's eight-row cap came from the fused decode select's shared memory
(~4.2 KB per row of fp8 q and weights; sixteen would exceed the block's
99 KB). Lifted by launching the select's rows in groups of eight (every
block scores a stripe of every row's own context, so a group is the same
work at any grouping and up to eight rows is the one launch it always
was), widening the pick kernels to sixteen request slots, sizing the DSA
layer's attention tiles and (row, split) workspace from the count, and
keeping the absorb/vout projections on their warp kernels for every decode
shape (an explicit `tensor_cores=false` from the decode path — the
tensor-core forms are tolerance-equal, not bitwise, and a batched row must
be bitwise the same row alone). Gates: `dsa_test` row groups (12 and 16
rows bitwise the host mirror), `glm_dsa_engine_test`'s sixteen-row world
(eight slots at depth 1, batched transcripts bitwise the eager engine's;
also 4 x 4 at depth 3 and 5 x 3 at depth 2), the full suite 78/78.

**Two things found on the way.** (1) The engine test's eager model was
configured for eight rows and the batched one for sixteen; every prompt of
9–16 tokens then prefilled through different kernels — the GEMM interface
lowers bf16 calls up to the model's decode rows to the row-independent
GEMV chain, above them cuBLASLt runs — and two of sixteen random-fixture
prompts flipped a near tie 7 and 30 tokens later. Deterministic within a
deployment; the gate configures both models alike; a sixteen-row
deployment prefills 9–16-token prompts through the GEMV chain where an
eight-row one uses cuBLASLt. (2) The first cut raised the projections'
tensor-core threshold from 16 to 32 rows instead of flagging the decode
path: the fabric rituals then reproduced only 1 of 4 reference transcripts
in both the T=1 and the depth-1 worlds, diverging at the same characters —
the prefix cache prefills the chat template's header as its own chunk of
16–31 rows, which had moved off the tensor cores. The flag replaces the
threshold; the rituals are rerun below.
